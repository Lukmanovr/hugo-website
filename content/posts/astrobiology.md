### [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2019.2201) / [PDF](/PDF/ast.2019.2201.pdf)

### TL;DR

In this paper we present:

- A new ultrafast optics line with beta-barium borate crystals in the beam delivery path — frequency doubling and tripling that deliver stable (shot-to-shot power jitter below 1%) far-UV femtosecond emission, well above the ionization threshold.
- A clear Precambrian chert — mostly SiO<sub>2</sub> glass with inclusions of absorptive kerogenous matter — as the testbed for the new laser frequencies.
- Moving from IR (775 nm) to UV (258 nm) improves the mass resolution of the measured spectra roughly tenfold, raises the total ion yield by another order of magnitude, and sharply reduces dispersion — the signal becomes largely insensitive to the heterogeneity of the analyte.
- A fast high-voltage module that removes selected abundant ions on their way to the detector, extending the detection range by a factor of 5–7.
- All of it on a coffee-cup-sized mass spectrometer built for in-situ measurements on the Moon, Mars, or Europa.
